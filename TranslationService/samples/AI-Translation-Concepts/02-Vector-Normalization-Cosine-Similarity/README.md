# 02 - Vector Normalization and Cosine Similarity

This module explores the mathematics of comparing embeddings: **Vector Normalization** and **Cosine Similarity**.

## What this concept is

Once we have converted text into numerical vectors (as seen in module 01), we need a way to determine how "close" or "similar" two vectors are. Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors in a multi-dimensional space. Normalization scales these vectors to have a length (magnitude) of exactly 1, allowing us to compute similarity using a simple dot product.

## Why it exists

In natural language processing, the magnitude of a vector often relates to the frequency of words rather than their underlying meaning. By normalizing vectors and measuring the angle between them (cosine similarity), we focus strictly on the *direction* of the vectors, which correlates to their semantic meaning, ignoring their magnitude.

## Where it is used

Every time a semantic search is performed, the system calculates the cosine similarity between the embedded query and the stored database embeddings to find the most relevant matches.

## Learning objectives

*   Understand the difference between a raw vector and a normalized vector.
*   Manually calculate a dot product.
*   Prove that the dot product of two normalized vectors is mathematically identical to their cosine similarity.
